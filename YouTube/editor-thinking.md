# Editor Thinking Framework

How a professional editor structures a long-form talking-head YouTube video. Extracted from reference study material 2026-06-04. Apply this every time a script is analyzed.

---

## The Three Zones of a Long-Form Video

Every long-form video has three zones. Animations and motion graphics serve a different purpose in each zone.

```
[ HOOK 0–60s ] → [ BODY ] → [ OUTRO/CTA ]
                    ↓
            [ Motion graphic inserts ]
            [ B-roll overlays ]
```

---

## Zone 1: Hook (0–60s)

**Goal:** Make the viewer decide to keep watching.

**What the editor does:**
1. **Shows main speaker** — open on the speaker's face. No title card. No intro. The face is the hook.
2. **Lower-half subtitles** — text sits in the lower third of the frame throughout the hook.
   - Regular speech → sans-serif font, normal weight, white/light color
   - Key words (the ones you'd stress out loud) → script or bold font + brand accent color + subtle glow/shadow
3. **Hook Message graphic** — one motion graphic insert that visualizes THE promise of the video. This is the one thing that answers "why should I keep watching?"

**Hook Message rules:**
- Only ONE hook message graphic per video
- It must be the boldest, most readable graphic in the entire video
- It appears within the first 30–45 seconds
- It directly mirrors what the speaker is saying at that moment

**Decision:** If the script's first 60 seconds contains a bold claim, a surprising number, or a "what if" — that's where the Hook Message goes.

---

## Zone 2: Body

The body is where you alternate between three treatments:

### A — Talking Head with Subtitles
No motion graphics. Just the speaker with lower-third subtitles following the dual-font rule:
- Regular sentences: sans-serif, normal weight
- Emphasis words: script/bold + brand accent color + glow

### B — Motion Graphic Inserts
Full-screen or 16:9 graphic that replaces the talking head for 3–8 seconds. Use when the speaker is explaining a concept that benefits from visualization.

**Animation type decision rules:**

| What the script says | Animation type to use |
|---|---|
| Numbered steps / a process / "here's how" | **Roadmap Card** — numbered boxes with arrows, sequential reveal |
| A money amount / stat / metric / percentage | **Stat Callout** — large number enters with count-up, subtitle below |
| A problem vs. solution / before vs. after / contrast | **Two-Panel Split** — left panel (problem) → right panel (solution) |
| A key quote or the main thesis of the video | **Quote Overlay** — full-screen text, key words in accent color |
| A list of 3–5 tips, features, or items | **Stacked Card Reveal** — cards slide in one by one, timed to speech |
| A single word or concept to really land | **Floating Text** — big type, accent color, glow pulse animation |
| Speaker references something happening on screen / "imagine" / "picture" | **B-roll Overlay** — minimal text over footage; one concept only |

### C — B-roll with Text Overlay
Footage plays. One short text label appears — one concept, minimal words. Never more than 5 words on screen at once during B-roll.

---

## Zone 3: Outro / CTA

**Goal:** Convert viewer into subscriber or next-video watcher.

**Treatments:**
- Subscribe card animation — simple, brand-colored
- Next video recommendation card
- Final CTA text overlay ("comment below with X" / "link in description")

**Rule:** The outro should take 15–30 seconds max. Don't let it drag.

---

## Universal Typography Rules

These apply everywhere — subtitles, overlays, motion graphics:

| Text type | Font style | Weight | Color | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Regular spoken text | Sans-serif | Regular | White / near-white | None |
| Emphasis / key words | Script or bold sans | Bold | Brand accent color | Subtle glow or color shadow |
| Graphic headlines | Bold sans or serif | Black/ExtraBold | White or accent | None (let size do the work) |
| Stat/number callouts | Bold sans | Black | White with accent subtitle | Count-up animation on enter |
| Card labels / tags | Sans-serif | Medium | Accent or muted | None |

---

## When NOT to Insert a Motion Graphic

- When the speaker is mid-story or building emotional tension — don't interrupt with graphics
- When two motion graphics would appear within 60 seconds of each other — space them out
- When the visual would be more confusing than clarifying — skip it, stay on talking head
- Never add graphics just to fill time — every graphic must serve the viewer's understanding

---

## Pacing Rules

- Hook graphic: appears within first 30–45s
- Average time between body motion graphics: 90–180 seconds
- Each graphic holds for 3–8 seconds max (unless it's a roadmap that builds over 10–15s)
- Outro begins no later than 30–45s before end of script

---

## How to Read a Script for Animation Opportunities

1. **Pass 1 — Hook scan:** Find the first bold claim, surprising number, or direct promise. Mark it as Hook Message.
2. **Pass 2 — Stat/number scan:** Highlight every dollar amount, percentage, time duration, or metric. Each one is a potential Stat Callout.
3. **Pass 3 — Process scan:** Find any sequence of steps, a before/after, or a "here's how." Each is a Roadmap or Two-Panel.
4. **Pass 4 — Quote scan:** Find the one sentence that best captures the video's core idea. That's the Quote Overlay.
5. **Pass 5 — List scan:** Find any group of 3–5 parallel items. That's a Stacked Card Reveal.
6. **Pass 6 — B-roll cues:** Find phrases like "imagine," "picture this," "look at this," or references to something physical/visual. Mark as B-roll Overlay.
7. **Final filter:** Remove any duplicates. If you have more than 1 graphic per 90 seconds, cut the weakest ones. Less is more.
